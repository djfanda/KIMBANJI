누구나, 언제나, 몇 번이고 같은 증명을 스스로 확인하는 반지입니다. 이 반지는 누구의 것도 아니고 우리 모두의 것입니다.

# KIMBANJI (김반지) — the proof ring for everyone

A ring anyone can use, any time and as often as they like, to check the same proofs for themselves. It belongs to no one and to all of us.

- Inside: 1,701 theorems in 60 modules, written in core Lean 4 only (no external library). They include the golden ratio identities for every n (((1+√5)/2)ⁿ = (Lₙ + Fₙ√5)/2, norm Lₙ² − 5Fₙ² = ±4, Cassini) and, for every prime p, Lₚ ≡ 1, Fₚ ≡ 5^((p−1)/2), quadratic reciprocity for 5 and Fₚ ≡ (p/5) (mod p).
  Added in the finale edition:
  · The silver ratio δ = 1 + √2 = (2 + √8)/2: norm Qₙ² − 2Pₙ² = (−1)ⁿ, 1/δ = δ − 2, the metallic means (n + √(n²+4))/2, Q_p ≡ 1 and P_p ≡ 2^((p−1)/2) (mod p) for every odd prime, and Gauss's second supplementary law (√2 lies in F_p ⇔ p ≡ ±1 (mod 8)) for every prime.
  · The doubling ladder: (1+i)⁴ = −4, (1+i)⁸ = 16, the two-, four- and eight-square identities (multiplicative norms of complex numbers, quaternions and octonions), all-ones norms 1, 2, 4, 8, quaternion non-commutativity, octonion non-associativity, and the reduction of complex Hermitian forms to real forms of twice the size (every n).
  · A PSD certificate checker and the interval-box theorem: one integer certificate for A₀ − n·e·I makes every integer matrix in the box of radius e positive semidefinite, decided by the kernel.
  · The Weil window √2 (even side, truncated to 6 dimensions), end to end: ball arithmetic → integer box (±1) → 51-digit certificate → kernel. It concerns the truncated subspace; the box inequality itself is supplied by Python ball arithmetic.
  · Seven balls: seven unit balls fit without overlap in a circle of radius 3 (Eisenstein integers); the vertices of each of the five Platonic solids lie on one sphere (golden integers, 1/φ² + φ² = 3); Euler V − E + F = 2.
  · Spheres from pairs: adding antipodal pairs (S⁰) one at a time gives two points, a circle, the octahedron surface (S²) and the 16-cell boundary (S³); face counts, χ = 2, 0, 2, 0, closed pseudo-manifolds, independence of the order, and integer homology decided by contraction certificates (d₂P + Qd₁ = I): H₁ = 0 for the octahedron, H₁ = H₂ = 0 for S³; as controls, the circle and the seven-vertex torus carry cycles that are not boundaries. Building spheres is not the Poincaré conjecture (the hard direction is Perelman's).
  · The binary icosahedral group: 120 unit quaternions with coordinates in (1/2)ℤ[φ]; closed under multiplication (all 14,400 products, from a generator certificate and associativity), inverses, centre ±1, perfect (every element is a product of commutators, so H₁ = 0), element orders. It is the group known as the fundamental group of the Poincaré homology sphere (the topology is named, not proved).
  · Exact fluid solutions: 2D Taylor–Green and 3D ABC flow (symbolic A, B, C) checked exactly as trigonometric polynomials with Gaussian-rational coefficients: zero divergence, Laplacian, Beltrami (curl U = U), nonlinear term equals a pressure gradient, every Navier–Stokes residual coefficient vanishes; a wrong pressure sign is rejected. The Millennium Navier–Stokes problem (3D regularity) is not addressed.
  · Mirror four: the four places {ρ, ρ̄, 1 − ρ, 1 − ρ̄} of a candidate zeta zero coincide in pairs exactly when the real part is 1/2, otherwise they are four distinct points; hence "every orbit collapses" ⇔ "every real part is 1/2" (a restatement of the Riemann hypothesis, not a proof).
  · Three axes, six slots: a status map of the seven problems on the faces and centre of a cube (author's reading); 24 rotations; after any rotation the infinite-field cells stay empty; no axis opens.
  · The Petersen star: outer pentagon and inner pentagram; exactly 120 automorphisms (S₅); vertex-transitive; no Hamiltonian cycle, but one after deleting any vertex; the quotient of the dodecahedron by ± pairs; no 3-edge-colouring.
  · Point counts on the Klein quartic: the projective count of x³y + y³z + z³x = 0 equals p + 1 − a_p(49a1)·(1 + χ(p) + χ(p)²) for all 77 primes 2 ≤ p < 400 other than 7 (χ the cubic character mod 7); extension fields F_{p^k} (k ≤ 3) and the degree-6 characteristic polynomial for p = 2, 3, 5. The splitting of the Jacobian is named, not proved; the Hodge conjecture in general is not addressed.
  · Algebraic certificates for 18 rank-two curves: the two Cremona generators lie on the curve; no small relations; independence in E(F_p)/2E(F_p) at a few primes per curve; root number +1 for the 15 semistable curves. The reduction homomorphism that turns this into "algebraic rank ≥ 2" is cited, and the analytic rank 2 remains a ball-arithmetic computation outside Lean.
  · Two tetrahedra: under polar duality the dual of T⁺ is T⁻; interpenetrating, their hull is a cube and their common core an octahedron; joined at a vertex they form an hourglass (one waist point, seven vertices); with positions fixed, all three axes are dual pairs.
  · Monge's line: for every integer input, the three external centres of similitude of three circles (and one external with two internal) are collinear (homogeneous determinant 0).
  Added in the 1,701 edition (three-axis experiments and the cube axis):
  · Sphere-recognition certificates: three triangulated 3-spheres (the join of two triangles, the boundary of the 16-cell, the join of a triangle and a hexagon) are reduced to ∂Δ⁴ by 2, 7 and 5 bistellar (Pachner) moves, checked by the kernel; manifold checks (every triangle in exactly two tetrahedra, every vertex link a 2-sphere); Euler characteristic 0; as controls, a suspended torus fails the link check and a reordered certificate is rejected. That a single move preserves the PL type is cited.
  · Exact counts for Z₂ lattice gauge theory: on the 2 × 2, 2 × 3 and 3 × 3 tori, all link configurations are counted (for 3 × 3, all 2^18 in thirty-two chunks): the number with k negative plaquettes is 2^{N+1} C(N, k) for even k and 0 for odd k, i.e. the coefficients of the partition function 2^N[(2 cosh β)^N + (2 sinh β)^N]; ker d₀ = 2, d₁ ∘ d₀ = 0, Betti numbers 1, 2, 1.
